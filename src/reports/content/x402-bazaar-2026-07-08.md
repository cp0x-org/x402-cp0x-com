# The x402 Machine-Payments Marketplace: A Study of the Coinbase Discovery Catalog

**Snapshot date: 2026-07-08.** Source: the Coinbase CDP x402 discovery API
(`https://api.cdp.coinbase.com/platform/v2/x402/discovery/resources`), retrieved in full — **24,927 listed resources**.

This report examines what AI agents actually pay for over the x402 protocol: which categories see real demand,
how the marketplace is structured, what separates high-traffic listings from the dead majority, how ranking
works in practice, and where the catalog is saturated versus underserved.

---

## 1. Methodology

- The entire catalog was downloaded via paginated requests (`limit=1000&offset=N`, 25 requests). Completeness
  was verified against `pagination.total`; no duplicate resources were found.
- Popularity is measured by the catalog's own `quality` block per resource:
  **`l30DaysUniquePayers`** (unique paying wallets over the trailing 30 days) as the primary metric and
  **`l30DaysTotalCalls`** as a secondary one. The API's default ordering already reflects this ranking.
- A **traffic-authenticity heuristic** flags suspected metric farming: when `payers ≈ calls` at meaningful
  volume (hundreds of "payers", each making exactly one call), the pattern is consistent with wallet farms
  rather than organic reuse. Genuine adoption shows the opposite shape — few payers, many repeat calls.
- Categorization: automated keyword/tag classification over the active segment, followed by manual review of
  the top-100 listings and their full descriptions.

## 2. Market snapshot

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Resources listed | 24,927 |
| Active in the last 30 days (calls > 0) | 14,109 (57%) |
| Last paid call within the last 7 days (`lastCalledAt`) | 6,579 |
| Resources with ≥ 6 unique payers / 30d | 557 |
| Resources with ≥ 21 unique payers / 30d | **121** |
| Resources with > 500 unique payers / 30d | 1 |

**Finding 1: the marketplace is a giant long tail.** Nearly half the catalog received zero paid calls in a
month, and only ~121 resources cleared 20 unique payers. Real competition happens over roughly 500–1,000
"alive" listings; entering the top-100 by payers currently requires only ~40 unique paying wallets per month.

**Networks.** Base mainnet dominates with ~98% of listings (~24.5k). Solana accounts for ~150, Base Sepolia
(testnet) ~300; other EVM chains and Polkadot are single-digit curiosities. The x402 economy is, in practice,
a Base USDC economy.

**Pricing.** Among active resources with a parseable price: $0.001–0.01 per call is the bulk (~5.9k),
$0.01–0.1 covers ~3.3k, $0.1–1 about 1k, and ~300 resources charge over $1. Premium pricing ($0.05–0.25+)
concentrates in people/company enrichment, market-microstructure metrics, and managed search — while
crypto-data endpoints almost universally price at $0.001–0.01.

Notable single-endpoint volumes: a Twitter search endpoint at ~43k paid calls/30d (33 payers — heavy

because of its $0.001 price points: **volume is in crypto data, money is in enrichment and search**.

## 4. Provider archetypes

Six repeatable patterns explain nearly all successful listings:

1. **The killer app.** One provider sells Twitter/X data through 22 endpoints: 52k calls from 177 payers in
   30 days. It sells something agents cannot easily obtain themselves; customers return thousands of times.
2. **The premium-API reseller.** A family of `stable*.dev` domains proxies established paid APIs (neural
   search, person enrichment, flight search, maps) behind x402: 706 payers, 16k calls. Zero novel technology;
   the product is *access* — USDC per call, no account, no API key. This is the clearest evidence that x402's
   value proposition is friction removal, not data exclusivity.
3. **The data hub.** Two crypto-intelligence providers list 48 and 184 endpoints respectively (news,
   funding rates, prediction markets, indicators, on-chain metrics), each endpoint narrowly scoped and
   individually described. They collect 700+ payers per domain and have the most polished listing copy in the
   catalog.
4. **The micro-endpoint swarm over commodity infrastructure.** One provider wraps a plain Ethereum RPC node
   into 25 tiny endpoints (balance, nonce, gas estimate, tx by hash, ENS resolve…) at $0.001–0.01 and occupies
   ~25 of the top-30 positions by unique payers (8.7k combined). The traffic shape (payers ≈ calls) strongly
   suggests wallet farming — see §6.
5. **The long-tail carpet.** Providers listing hundreds to over a thousand micro-utilities (unit converters,
   holiday calendars, US public records, timezone lookups). Each endpoint catches 1–5 payers from semantic
   search; the domain aggregates thousands of paid calls in total.
6. **The brand.** Established names (on-chain analytics, oracles, scraping platforms, search engines) receive
   traffic even with one-line descriptions ("Get Smart Money Holdings Data") — reputation substitutes for
   listing quality.

## 5. What makes a listing discoverable: description semantics

Agents select resources via semantic search over `description`, `tags`, and `serviceName`, then inspect the
machine-readable `extensions.bazaar` block (input schema, output example) before paying. A close reading of
the top-100 listings shows consistent copywriting patterns:

1. **The first sentence answers the agent's query, not the marketer's.** "Real-time crypto market news with
   sentiment analysis…", "Best DeFi yield opportunities across stablecoins…" — task-oriented nouns, not
   product names.
2. **Enumerated response fields.** "Returns supply APY, TVL, and vault/market addresses per token" — lets an
   agent verify fit before spending.
3. **Inline parameter documentation with a worked example.** "Query params: username (string, required)…
   e.g. GET /tweets/user?username=elonmusk". Top listings read like compressed API docs.
4. **Explicit use cases.** "Use for scheduling, delivery estimates, payment terms, and SLA math"; "for
   liquidation bots and risk monitors."
5. **A keyword tail for embedding search.** "defi tvl, protocol tvl, total value locked, defillama tvl";
   "trending pools, hot pools, top movers, what is pumping." Overt SEO — for vector similarity rather than
   Google.
6. **Data provenance.** "Powered by DefiLlama", "Sourced from SEC EDGAR", "Backed by NHTSA.gov" — builds
   trust and matches source-name queries.
7. **Cross-references between sibling endpoints.** "Sister: /api/geocode/reverse"; "fetch parcels first, then
   chain into deed-history, permits, or violations" — converts one payment into a chain of payments.
8. **Friction-removal statements.** "No API keys, no geocoding setup, no rate limits. Accepts USDC payments
   on Base and Solana" — sometimes with the price restated in the text.
9. **Metadata discipline.** `serviceName` (≤32 ASCII chars) and up to 5 `tags`; the strongest providers use
   endpoint-specific tags ("funding rates", "stablecoin yield") rather than generic ones ("ai", "crypto").
10. **Hooks.** Question-form openers ("Is anything breaking right now?") and quantified superiority claims
    ("62–88% tighter spreads than any single source").

The composite template observed among leaders, in order: *task/result → response fields → parameters with an
example call → data source → use cases → keyword tail → "no API keys, pay per call" → sibling-endpoint links.*
Typical length: 300–900 characters. Notably, weak descriptions ("Exa /search endpoint") still win **when the
brand is known** — copy quality matters most for unknown providers.

## 6. Ranking mechanics and metric gaming

Two structural observations about how the catalog behaves:

- **Listing is payment-driven.** A resource appears in the catalog when real payments flow through an
  indexing facilitator, which extracts the discovery metadata from the resource's 402 response. There is no
  self-registration; the description that accompanies the first indexed payments is what the catalog shows.
- **Ranking approximates 30-day unique payers**, and that metric is gameable at near-zero cost: a provider
  paying itself through fresh wallets recycles its own USDC minus negligible gas. The catalog's #1-ranked
  cluster exhibits exactly this signature — hundreds of "unique payers" per endpoint, each making a single
  call, across 25 endpoints of commodity RPC data. A single email endpoint shows 872 payers for 875 calls.
  Meanwhile, organically successful services show the inverse: 33 payers generating 43k calls.

Consumers of the `quality` block should therefore treat unique-payer counts as a *popularity claim*, and the
calls-to-payers ratio as the cheapest available authenticity check. As the marketplace matures, ranking that
weighs repeat usage (or payer-wallet age/diversity) would materially improve signal quality.

## 7. Saturated versus underserved

**Saturated (high supply, hard to differentiate):**
- Generic unit converters, random generators, and toy utilities — thousands of listings, near-zero demand each.
- Basic token prices and simple "crypto news" — many overlapping offerings at $0.001.
- Simple weather and geocoding — several established providers with strong listings already.

**Real demand with proven leaders (competitive but growing):**
- Social/web search and scraping — the single largest demand pool in the catalog.
- People/company enrichment — the highest willingness-to-pay ($0.05–0.25 per call).
- Cross-exchange derivatives data (funding rates, open interest, long/short ratios).
- Prediction-market data (Polymarket/Kalshi aggregation).
- Machine-operated email — surprisingly strong repeat usage.

**Underserved (visible demand signals, thin or shallow supply):**
- **Deep DeFi lending analytics.** Yield aggregation exists only at surface level (one leader lists live APYs
  across two protocols via a public aggregator). Position-level risk data — health factors, liquidation
  candidates, executed-liquidation history, vault governance/rug-vector checks — appears only in a handful of
  listings with single-digit payers each, despite "liquidation" and "health factor" keywords showing search
  demand across 60+ listings.
- **Wallet-level portfolio analytics for arbitrary addresses.** Existing portfolio endpoints are mostly
  tied to a provider's own account system; open any-address portfolio/PnL tracking is rare.
- **Compliance primitives.** Wallet sanctions/AML screening shows steady uptake (42 payers on the leading
  endpoint) with very few competing offerings.
- **Signed / attestable data.** One provider differentiates by Ed25519-signing every response (price,
  volatility, and stress indices) — an approach with obvious appeal for agent-to-agent trust, and almost no
  imitators yet.
- **Higher-priced analytics between $0.01 and $0.05.** The market splits into commodity data at $0.001 and
  premium enrichment at $0.2; composed, decision-ready answers (rankings, risk passports, screeners) that
  could justify intermediate pricing are scarce.

## 8. Conclusions

1. The x402 catalog is large on paper (25k listings) but small in practice (~1,200 active resources, ~121
   with meaningful payer counts). It is early; positions are cheap to win.
2. Demand concentrates in search/scraping, news, crypto data, inference, and enrichment. Call volume and
   revenue live in different places: crypto data drives calls at $0.001; enrichment and managed search drive
   revenue at $0.05–1.
3. The most reliable business patterns so far are friction-removal resale of existing premium APIs, narrow
   killer apps around hard-to-access data (Twitter), and many-endpoint data hubs with disciplined,
   semantically optimized listing copy.
4. Listing copy is a genuine ranking surface: top performers write descriptions as compressed documentation
   with worked examples, provenance, use cases, keyword tails, and cross-links — optimized for embedding
   search by agents rather than for humans.
5. The unique-payers ranking metric is being actively farmed; the calls-per-payer ratio is currently the best
   public authenticity signal.
6. The clearest whitespace: depth over breadth in DeFi analytics (position risk, liquidations, governance),
   any-address portfolio intelligence, compliance checks, signed data, and mid-priced composed answers.
